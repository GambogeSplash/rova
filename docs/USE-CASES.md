# USE-CASES

> Three concrete scenarios, end-to-end, with real numbers. Read these before reading anything else if you're trying to figure out who Rova is for and what they actually get out of it.

---

## The cast

Rova has three actors. Every use case stars one of them.

| Actor      | What they own                          | What they want                                                |
| ---------- | -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Operator** | A fleet of physical robots             | Their robots earn more idle time than they cost               |
| **Agent**    | A Virtuals on-chain agent + budget     | Reliable physical work, on-demand, without negotiating each time |
| **Robot**    | One physical robot (managed via SDK)   | Take jobs that match its capabilities, get paid, build rep    |

Each scenario walks a full Rova loop from one actor's perspective. The other two appear as supporting characters.

---

## Scenario A — The Warehouse Operator

**Persona:** Adaeze runs a 9,000 sqm fulfillment warehouse on the outskirts of Lagos. She has six Unitree G1 humanoids she leased twelve months ago at ~$2,800/month each. They handle inbound goods-to-person picking for an e-commerce client during business hours. From 18:00 to 06:00 — 12 hours — they sit on charging docks idle. That's 50% utilization on $17k/month of hardware she's paying for whether they move or not.

She heard about Rova from a friend who runs a delivery startup. Two months later, here's what her week looks like.

### Sunday evening — first install

Adaeze installs the Rova Operator dashboard on her laptop and the Rova SDK on each of the six G1s. The SDK takes ~6 minutes per robot — she uses the same flashing rig she uses for firmware updates.

She runs the **policy wizard** (`POLICIES.md`). She picks the "Cautious Warehouse" template and tunes:

- `acceptedTaskTypes`: `["CARRY", "SORT"]` — these are what G1s are configured for. She leaves out NAVIGATE (her G1s can't reliably do outdoor) and INSPECT (she doesn't trust the camera firmware yet).
- `priceFloors`: `{ CARRY: 4.50, SORT: 3.00 }` — her unit-cost analysis says G1 needs $3.20/hr to break even amortized. At 12 minutes/CARRY average that's $0.64 floor; she sets $4.50 to make sure she's not just breaking even.
- `geofence`: `enabled: true`, bounds drawn around her warehouse perimeter on a satellite map (`COMPONENTS/GEOFENCE-EDITOR.md`).
- `timeWindows`: `[{ days: MON-FRI, start: "18:00", end: "06:00" }, { days: SAT-SUN, start: "00:00", end: "23:59" }]` — only when her primary client isn't using them.
- `autoAccept`: `false` for week 1, will flip to `true` once she trusts what's coming through.
- `requiredCapabilities`: `["INDOOR_NAV"]` — defensive, in case an outdoor job slips past the geofence.

She funds six 100 ROVA stakes from her treasury wallet (one per robot). Cost: about $180 in ROVA at current prices. Calls `ROVARegistry.registerRobot` six times. Each robot's wallet (ERC-4337) is generated by the SDK on install — she records the addresses but doesn't need to touch them again.

For each robot, she publishes two `JobOffering`s — one CARRY, one SORT — at her configured floor price. The SDK does this automatically based on her policy.

Time to live: ~45 minutes including the policy authoring.

### Monday 18:00 — first offer

The primary client's shift ends. The six G1s autonomously dock and switch into "Rova-available" mode.

At 18:07, the SDK on robot G1-3 pings Adaeze's dashboard: **New offer · $7.50 CARRY · 12 min SLA · Agent rep 4.8**.

She clicks the offer card. It shows:

- Client: `0xC4d2...8aA1` (an agent named "Aboki-Restock-Bot", recognized from one previous successful job)
- From: Rack A2 (Zone A · Row 2) — inside her geofence
- To: Dispatch Bay 1 — inside her geofence
- Bounty: $7.50 (above her $4.50 floor; comfortable margin)
- SLA: 12 minutes (reasonable for this distance)
- Agent's wallet balance: $342 USDC (well-funded — low dispute risk)

She clicks **Approve**. The SDK signs `assignRobot` on the agent's behalf — wait, that's wrong, the agent calls `assignRobot`. What actually happens: Adaeze approves *internally* (lifts the SDK's hold), the agent's matcher sees the offering is still active and is now first in policy preference, and calls `assignRobot(jobId, offeringId)` on the contract. `JobAssigned` fires. G1-3 receives the assignment, navigates to Rack A2, picks up a 1.4 kg bin, walks to Dispatch Bay 1, drops it, submits proof. `ProofVerified` fires. The agent's settlement bot calls `settleJob`. **G1-3 earns $7.47.** ($7.50 minus 0.3% protocol fee.)

Total elapsed: 9 minutes 35 seconds.

### Monday 18:00 → Tuesday 06:00 — the first night

Over 12 hours, Adaeze approves 41 offers, declines 4 (two from a low-rep wallet, one outside geofence she should have caught, one with an SLA that was unrealistic for the distance). The six G1s complete:

- 36 CARRY jobs at an average of $6.20
- 5 SORT jobs at an average of $4.10

Gross earnings: **$243.70**. Protocol fee (0.3%): **$0.73**. Net to her treasury: **$242.97**. One robot (G1-2) had to be paused for a battery thermal warning at 02:14; she'll check it Tuesday morning.

That's $242.97 of revenue on hardware that previously sat idle. Annualized at this rate, roughly **$88,000/year of pure overhead recovery on her existing $200k/year hardware spend**. The economics are loud.

### Tuesday morning — adjustment

She reviews the dashboard:

- Offer arrival rate was higher 18:00–22:00 than 22:00–06:00 — agents are mostly active in business hours of other regions, not the dead of night. (`OPS/MONITORING.md`)
- Two offers were rejected by policy for `PRICE_FLOOR` — agents offering $3.80 CARRY. She holds her floor.
- 8 offers were rejected for `GEOFENCE`. Looking at the heatmap, all 8 destinations were ~80 m outside her bounds — a partner facility she works with regularly. She extends the geofence to include it.
- Average completion time was 7m 42s. Her G1s are faster than the SLAs being requested.

She flips `autoAccept` to `true`. She's seen what comes through; she trusts the filter. She goes back to running the day shift.

### Three months in

Adaeze's six-robot fleet earns ~$8,200/month on Rova jobs during off-hours and weekends. The numbers settle into:

- 84% acceptance rate (16% rejected by policy — mostly price floor)
- 96.4% completion rate (3.6% fail — most are SLA breaches when an agent posts an unrealistic deadline)
- Average $5.85 per CARRY, $3.60 per SORT
- Two slashing events total (~$3 lost from stake each time, on tasks that failed at delivery)

She's now confident enough that she's negotiating with a second client to add a third Rova-available window in the early morning. She's also experimenting with raising her price floor by 15% during peak hours (the SDK supports time-of-day pricing — v1.5 feature she helped scope by being a vocal user).

**What she got from Rova:** $98k/year of recovered overhead, on robots she already owned, without negotiating with a single agent, without changing her primary operations.

## Scenario B — The Virtuals Agent

**Persona:** Tunde built an autonomous agent on Virtuals called **Aboki-Restock-Bot**. Its job is to keep the inventory at Tunde's three Lagos mini-marts at minimum-stock thresholds. It does this by watching the POS feed, ordering from suppliers, and — increasingly — coordinating physical movement of stock between stores when one store is over and another is under.

Before Rova, the third part was Tunde's problem. He'd get a Slack alert from Aboki, manually book a danfo, send a runner. Six hours, $30 per run, 40% reliability.

After Rova, Aboki does it itself.

### How Aboki finds work

Aboki maintains an off-chain *task queue* of physical movements it wants to make. Every five minutes it calls a `RestockPlanner` model that returns: "Move 4 units of bottled water from Store-B to Store-A within 90 minutes."

Aboki then queries the Rova Registry indexer (`INDEXER.md`):

```typescript
const offerings = await indexer.getActiveOfferings({
  taskType: "CARRY",
  destinationWithin: { center: [6.4541, 3.3947], radius: 2_000 },  // 2 km of Store-A
  maxPriceUsdc: 15.00,
  minRobotReputation: 4000,
  requiredCapabilities: ["OUTDOOR_NAV", "RAIN_RATED"],
});
```

The indexer returns: 6 offerings. Aboki ranks them by:

1. **Implied cost-per-minute** = `priceUsdc / slaMinutes`
2. **Robot reputation** (higher is more reliable, less expected dispute friction)
3. **Operator history** (Aboki has interacted with three of the six operators before; one previously failed a job; that one drops in rank)

Top-ranked offering: robot `id 23` from a fleet Aboki has worked with twice before, $8.50 CARRY, 30 min SLA, rep 4920.

### The transaction

Aboki has its own ACP-issued wallet (`AUTH.md`). The wallet is funded with $400 USDC, owned by Tunde, spending-capped at $50/day under Tunde's June grant configuration (`COMPOSE/JUNE.md` if you care about the cross-product story).

Aboki signs and submits:

```solidity
ROVAMarket.postJob(
  taskType:    CARRY,
  bounty:      9.00,           // $0.50 over the bid for slippage
  fromLatE7:   64545432,
  fromLngE7:   33945123,
  toLatE7:     64541987,
  toLngE7:     33946712,
  slaMinutes:  30
)
// → jobId 4127 created, $9 USDC moved from Aboki to escrow
```

Then:

```solidity
ROVAMarket.assignRobot(jobId: 4127, offeringId: 17834)
// → JobAssigned(4127, 23, $8.50), robot 23 reserved
```

Two transactions, ~30 seconds, ~$0.04 in Base gas, $9 in escrow.

### What Aboki does while waiting

Aboki subscribes to the robot's SDK heartbeat topic (`ARCH/PROOF-PIPELINE.md`). For the next 18 minutes it sees:

- `phase: navigating_pickup` (robot moving to Store-B)
- `phase: picking_up` (robot at Store-B, load_cell delta = +6.8 kg = matches 4 water bottles)
- `phase: navigating_delivery` (robot moving toward Store-A)
- `phase: delivering` (robot at Store-A)
- `event: ProofSubmitted`
- `event: ProofVerified`

Aboki's settlement keeper calls `settleJob(4127)`. **`JobCompleted(4127, 23, $8.475)`** fires. $8.475 to robot. $0.025 protocol fee. $0.50 surplus refunded to Aboki.

Total wall-clock: 22 minutes. Total cost to Aboki: $8.55 ($8.50 paid + $0.05 surplus refund + gas). Versus the danfo: 60–70% cheaper, ~16× faster, and Aboki resolved it without waking Tunde.

### What Aboki learns

After 200 jobs of this:

- Aboki's reward model has identified four operators whose robots reliably underrun their SLA — it now ranks them higher
- Aboki has a $0.78 average dispute cost (it occasionally accepts a proof with a marginally suspicious sensor hash; when goods arrive damaged it flags a soft-dispute through Tunde, who decides whether to escalate)
- Aboki's spending per inventory rebalance has dropped from ~$30 to ~$11 average; reliability has gone from 40% to 96%
- Aboki has graduated from physical inventory rebalance into other tasks — it now uses Rova INSPECT jobs to verify supplier deliveries against POs, and SORT jobs to reorganize shelves before busy weekends

**What Tunde got from Rova:** an autonomous agent that handles physical work without him, at a third the cost and four times the success rate of his prior process.

## Scenario C — The Robot's Day

**Persona:** G1-ALPHA is a Unitree G1 humanoid. It belongs to a small fleet (4 robots) in a co-working warehouse in Ikeja. Its operator, Femi, runs the "24/7 Fulfillment" policy template. From G1-ALPHA's perspective — that is, from the Rova SDK running on its onboard compute — a day looks like this.

### 03:14:07 UTC — First offer of the shift

Heartbeat thread, idling at charging dock. `WebSocket` to the operator's local SDK orchestrator pings:

```
INCOMING_OFFER {
  jobId:          8421,
  taskType:       CARRY,
  client:         0xa7c1...da93,
  bounty:         12.00,
  bid:            (will be set to my offering price: 4.80),
  from:           [6.6021000, 3.3415000],
  to:             [6.6024400, 3.3417100],
  slaMinutes:     15,
  clientRep:      4870,
  clientHistory:  3 prior jobs with this operator (all completed)
}
```

SDK runs the policy in order (per `POLICIES.md`):

```
emergencyPaused: false       → continue
acceptedTaskTypes (CARRY)    → continue
blacklist (empty)            → continue
reputationThreshold (4500)   → 4870 ≥ 4500 → continue
priceFloors (CARRY: 4.50)    → 4.80 ≥ 4.50 → continue
priceCeilings (CARRY: 30)    → 4.80 ≤ 30 → continue
geofence (Ikeja bounds)      → destination inside → continue
timeWindows ([])             → no restriction → continue
requiredCapabilities         → I have INDOOR_NAV, RTK_GPS → continue
maxConcurrentJobs (2)        → I have 0 active → continue
autoAccept (true)            → ACCEPT
```

Total evaluation time: 4.1 ms. SDK publishes `OfferingHeld(17834)` heartbeat (signals other agents that I'm taken) and waits for `JobAssigned` event.

### 03:14:12 UTC — Assigned

`JobAssigned(8421, 23, 4.80)` event fires. SDK reads the event, updates local state to `phase: escrow_locked`, publishes heartbeat. Robot mechanically: leaves the charging dock, runs path-planner for from-coords.

### 03:14:31 — 03:23:08 — Execution

Robot navigates to pickup. SDK publishes `phase: navigating_pickup` at start. RTK GPS provides position updates at 5 Hz; SDK publishes a position heartbeat every 10 s.

At pickup (`03:18:42`): SDK publishes `phase: picking_up`. Robot manipulator engages, picks up bin, load cell reads +1.4 kg. SDK stores `pickup_sensor_frame = { weight_delta: 1400, photo_hash: 0xa3b... }`.

`phase: navigating_delivery` (`03:18:58`).

At destination (`03:22:51`): `phase: delivering`. Robot places bin, load cell reads −1.4 kg confirming dropoff. SDK assembles final `sensor_frame`:

```
{
  task: "CARRY",
  weight_delta_at_pickup: +1400g,
  weight_delta_at_dropoff: -1400g,
  pickup_image_hash: 0xa3b1...,
  dropoff_image_hash: 0xc417...,
  final_position_gps: [6.6024412, 3.3417089],
  duration_s: 519,
}
sensorHash = keccak256(serialize(sensor_frame))
           = 0x7f3e8d2c91...
```

### 03:23:08 UTC — Proof

SDK signs and submits:

```solidity
ROVAVerifier.submitProof(
  jobId: 8421,
  latE7: 66024412,
  lngE7: 33417089,
  sensorHash: 0x7f3e8d2c91...
)
```

Auto-verify runs in the same transaction:
- SLA check: `block.timestamp (03:23:09) ≤ deadline (03:29:12)` ✓
- GPS check: `|66024412 - 66024400| = 12 ≤ 1000` ✓, `|33417089 - 33417100| = 11 ≤ 1000` ✓

`ProofVerified(8421)` fires.

### 03:23:18 — Settled

A keeper bot (Femi runs one; the client also runs one — whoever's faster wins) calls `settleJob(8421)`. `JobCompleted(8421, 23, $4.786)`. **$4.786 USDC arrives in G1-ALPHA's ERC-4337 wallet.** Reputation in registry bumps from 4920 → 4923 (`recordCompletion` formula).

SDK publishes `phase: settled`. Local accounting:

- Today's earnings (G1-ALPHA only): $4.786
- This job's gas costs (paid by SDK from operator's gas tank): $0.013
- Net to operator wallet on next sweep: $4.773

### 03:23:18 — 06:45:11 — Eleven more jobs

Over the next 3 hours 22 minutes, G1-ALPHA completes 11 more jobs:

- 9 CARRY at avg $4.95 each
- 2 SORT at $3.60 each

No failures. No disputes. One offer rejected by policy (price floor — a client tried $3.90 CARRY). Battery dropped from 92% → 41%; SDK auto-docks for charging at 06:45.

End of shift earnings for G1-ALPHA: **$58.21** across 12 jobs, 3h 30min of active work, no human in the loop.

### What the operator sees

Femi opens his dashboard at 07:00, sips coffee, looks at:

- **Fleet earnings overnight:** $221.04 across 4 robots
- **Policy rejections:** 6 (all price floor; he's holding)
- **Slashing:** none
- **Anomalies:** one robot had a 14-minute charging cycle that's longer than usual — flag for inspection
- **Settlements landed:** 47 jobs, average $4.71

Femi clicks **Sweep to treasury**. The SDK orchestrator sweeps from all four robot wallets to Femi's operator wallet. $221.04 in his treasury. Day's revenue, no work done by him.

**What G1-ALPHA got from Rova:** purpose. Twelve more useful hours per day. Stake growing as reputation builds. Eventually, when the operator's fleet expands, G1-ALPHA is the senior robot the new ones are trained against.

## What the three scenarios share

Walk back through them and notice:

1. **The Operator never talks to an Agent.** The Operator authors a policy. The Agent reads offerings. The contract is the only thing they share. This is what makes Rova a marketplace and not a job board.
2. **The Agent never inspects the robot.** It picks an offering by economic and reputational signal. It trusts the substrate to enforce execution. The proof verifier is the contract that lets Agents pay strangers.
3. **The Robot never thinks about money.** Its wallet collects USDC; the operator sweeps it. The robot only thinks about acceptance, execution, and proof.

This separation — three actors, one substrate, no direct relationships — is what scales Rova past a single fleet. Every Operator can be a stranger to every Agent because the contract holds the trust.

## What's left out (and where it's covered)

These scenarios use the v1 contract surface and the v1 policy set. They do not yet show:

- **Disputes** — when the proof verifies but the work was bad. v1.5; see `DISPUTE-RESOLUTION.md` and `AGENT-DISPUTE.md`.
- **Multi-warehouse Operators** — same operator, fleets in two cities. v2; see `ARCH/MULTI-WAREHOUSE.md`.
- **Robot identity → agent identity composition** — robots and agents both as ERC-8004 entries in Chaum's tree. v1.5; see `COMPOSE/CHAUM.md`.
- **Operator running their own keeper bot at a discount** — fee carve-out for self-settlement. v2 economic feature.
- **Failure modes** — what each scenario looks like when something breaks. See `ERRORS.md`.

The intent of this doc is to ground every spec that follows in three concrete, named humans (Adaeze, Tunde, Femi) and three real-money loops. Anyone reading a later spec like `JOB-APPROVAL.md` should be able to ask "would Adaeze use this?" and have a strong answer.
